## Confirmatory tests, cohort 1 (pre-registered H1/H3/H4), 2026-08-31

Common 8-class scheme (`wash_category_mapping.cls`) recomputed on both A and B (deviation §7: v2 CLOB tags don't reproduce A's 12 fine classes; both sides aggregated to the common 8). **Fee-free control confirmed present in B: geopolitics (31% fee-paying vs 94–100% elsewhere).**

**Overriding caveat — migration/fee conflation.** B is v2 (post-28-Apr migration); A-April is late v1. So B-vs-A(April) conflates the *fee rollout* with the *v1→v2 migration*. The migration moved market structure a lot, and it dominates cohort 1. The fee-specific signal must come from the DiD (which nets out the common migration shock via the control) and from cohorts 2–6, not from raw B-vs-A levels.

**H3 (provider structure unchanged) — not confirmed as levels, but the change is migration, not fees.** Only 1/7 fee-paying classes have maker HHI+top-5 within ±25 % of A-April (crypto, the one class dominated by the same ephemeral up/down bots in both regimes). Others shifted (finance ×2.4, esports ×1.9, politics ×0.1). This is the v1→v2 maker-base change, not a fee effect.

**H4 (order size lower under fees) — not confirmed; the drop is uniform, i.e. not fee-driven.** Log taker-order size fell ~2.1–3.0 log points from A(Oct–Dec) to B in *every* class — including the fee-free control geopolitics (−2.9). The DiD (treated − control) is **+0.28 log points (wrong sign, n.s.; SE undefined with a single clean control)** — so fee classes did **not** shrink more than the fee-free control. The uniform collapse is the v2 ephemeral-market explosion (order sizes tiny platform-wide), not the fee.

**H1a (round trips don't rise) — mixed, magnitudes small.** 4/7 fee-paying classes within ±1 pp of A-April; round-trip share rose modestly in sports (+1.2 pp) and finance (+2.1 pp), fell in esports (−1.1 pp). No large wash increase.

**H1c (self-matching) — confirmed structurally: 0 of 84.3 M fills** (the v2 CLOB never self-crosses).

**Reading.** Cohort 1's fee-specific inference is *consistent with Sample A's nulls*: netting out the migration (DiD, within-B control), fees show **no** differential effect on provider structure or order size, and only small, mixed round-trip changes — fees are not creating integrity problems. The large level shifts are the migration (Paper C's domain). This validates the two-step / multi-cohort design: one v2 cohort against v1 cannot separate fee from migration; cohorts 2–6 (all v2, fee vs fee-free within-regime) will. **H2 (negRisk no-arbitrage band — the one positive integrity result on A) is the remaining confirmatory test**, pending the family price-sum computation (CLOB `neg_risk_market_id` groups families).

**Partially consistent with the A result.** The fee-family band **0.0200 sits exactly in the predicted (0.012, 0.031] range** — wider than A's fee-free v1 baseline (0.012) and below A's fee level (0.031), i.e. the band-widening from A replicates directionally in v2 fee families. But the *within-B* ordering (fee > fee-free) does **not** hold (0.020 < 0.030) because the v2 fee-free negRisk control is tiny and illiquid (818 hours, p90 0.68) — fees are near-universal on v2 multi-candidate markets, so there's no usable within-v2 fee-free negRisk control. The clean test is B-fee vs A-fee-free (0.020 > 0.012 ✓).
